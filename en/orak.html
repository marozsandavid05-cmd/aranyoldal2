A pocket watch from grandfather’s waistcoat, a wristwatch from the drawer, a clock from the display cabinet. We look at running and stopped watches alike, and tell you whether the movement or the material is worth more. Do not have it repaired first.Bring the old watch as it is: a repair often costs more than it adds to the value.

What we look at on a watch

Five things decide what it is worth. We look at each one in front of you, and we only open the case with your permission.

  • I

    The case material

    Gold, silver, gold-plated or steel. The hallmark is inside the lid or on the case back, and we can also value a gold watch by the gram.

  • II

    The movement

    Who made it, whether it is hand-wound or automatic, and whether it has a special complication such as a repeater or a moon phase.

  • III

    The dial

    An original, undamaged dial is worth a lot. If we see a crack or a repaint, we will show you.

  • IV

    The condition

    A stopped or damaged watch can still be worth a lot. A missing glass or a broken hand is no reason to throw it away.

  • V

    What comes with it

    The original box, the papers, the chain or the key. If you have them, bring them too.

From the great houses to small workshops

We buy Swiss, Austrian, German and Hungarian watches too. If you do not know what watch you have, send us a phone photo of it or call us, and we will tell you where to look for the maker’s name.
